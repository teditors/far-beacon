# Versions

From v0.6 on, each version is a frozen branch named `version/v0.X`. On GitHub, open the branch menu (it says
`main`) and pick one to see the whole project as it was, including the playable `dist/index.html`. `main` is always
the latest. Version branches are never changed after they are made. The two early prototypes are kept as files in `prototypes/`.

| Version | What it added |
| --- | --- |
| v0.1 | 2D turn-based prototype of the expedition loop (`prototypes/2d/`) |
| v0.2 | First-person grey-box walk test with water pressure (`prototypes/walk-test/`) |
| v0.6 | The 3D game as of 13 Nov 2026: survival, biomes and ground types, generated lost peoples, characters and persistent worlds, levels and equipment, combat, towns, merchants and the rolled quest. Rebuilt as modules with identical behaviour |
| v0.8 | Towns and the locked place are places you walk into. The town has a gate, a square, a well, and the elder, the inn and the shop as people you walk up to. The locked place is three dark levels of rooms and corridors with guardians, store-rooms and traps, stairs between them, and the relic on the last level; you carry it back out. Filling water stops at what you can carry. Lanterns cost 8 |
| v0.9 | The world is nine regions in a three-by-three grid that wraps around: walk off any edge of a region and you arrive on the facing edge of the next, and keep going one way and you come home. Each region has its own biome, creatures, water, ruins and caves, built the first time you reach it; regions further from home are more dangerous. The quest stays in the home region. The Map screen shows the world. Old saves become the home region of a new world |
| v0.10 | Land agrees across region borders: what counts as a mountain is measured over the whole world, biomes blend into each other near a border, and rivers and roads cross borders at points both sides share, so they carry on into the next region. Buildings and creatures stand on the ground: sites get a level pad as wide as what stands there |
| v0.11 | You can see across borders: the eight regions around you are built ahead and drawn out to the limit of sight, so hills, forests, ruins and the beacon are visible in the next region and nothing changes as you step over. The town stands in the land as a real walled town, seen from far off by its hearth smoke; you walk in through the gate in its south wall and out again, with no button and no jump. Its 5 x 5 tiles can no longer be walked on outdoors; old saves move anything left there to the gate |
| v0.12 | The first commission: fifteen pieces, each with its own switch. A generated Almanac of everything the game rolls; every world has a year (drought, hard winter, rarely two moons or a wide world); gods rolled from a gift, a cost and a vow; three named artifacts per world; rare regions; creature specials; exotic races and classes; town trades and customs, and rarely a town of the people who never fell; errands at the inn, with liars; odd weather; generated biomes; odd levels in the locked place; relics that do something; lairs; richer finds far from home. See `JOURNAL.md` |
| v0.13 | The second commission. Towns are open (walls are rare and have several gates), range from hamlet to city, and are full of things: streets, a healer, a temple, a trainer, a smith, a mapmaker, townsfolk with rumours. Each world offers 2 to 12 races and 8 to 20 classes, with 45 hybrids and fight moves. The quest's places lie across a chain of regions, so the game is a journey. The map is one continuous world with pan and zoom. Hamlets in the country, finer items far from home, rare countries that change the land. See `JOURNAL.md` |
| v0.14 | The third commission. A performance meter and graphics quality (Low / Medium / High), with saves a third the size and no hitch at borders; worlds from 2 x 3 to 7 x 7; up to six towns; a great trouble per world (relic, beast, curse, sickness, cult, siege) and side troubles in other towns; crafting; trade between towns; a shaded sky and weather. See `JOURNAL.md` |
| v0.15 | The fourth commission. God mode and world settings; dungeons below the world in six kinds; ways to fly and places in the sky; seas, lakes, boats and places under the water; rare doors to other worlds; troubles that go below, above, under and beyond, with a goal line that finds the way; wayfarers in the wilds. See `JOURNAL.md` |
| v0.16 | Commission 5: how the game looks and sounds. A painted storybook style: smooth land in warm sun and cool shade, a deepening sky and blue moonlight; textures made in code; trees with shapes that sway; towns built by their climate, trade and custom, glowing at night; levels with walls of their own; ruins in their people's style, aged and marked by their doom; landmarks; people and creatures that walk, breathe, look round, lunge and flinch; campfires, springs and shadows; real-time shadows on High; a generative score that follows where you are and the sounds of the world. Every piece has a switch in Menu, Settings; Low and Medium are cheaper a frame than before. |
| v0.17 | Commission 6: an art direction. Painted atmosphere: every place has a palette rolled from its climate, the world, the year, troubles, curses, rare countries, the weather and the hour; distance fades in steps into bands of far hills; rare worlds in sepia or all reds. Toon touches: soft bands of light and thin tinted outlines on people, creatures, buildings and things. Other worlds in clean line: ink on everything, pale flat palettes of their own, empty skies, music of their own. Old High is the new Low, with new Medium and High above it and a hidden Fallback that comes by itself when frames stay slow; at most 60 frames a second, 30 with Save battery. ART.md is the style guide; pictures before and after in docs/look/. |
| v0.18 | Commission 7: rooms full of things. Every building in a town is a place you walk into through its open door, with rooms that suit it (a house's living room, bedroom, kitchen and workroom; the inn's taproom; the elder's hall; the temple's chapel; the smithy), furnished by the town's trade, size, climate and the year. Ninety kinds of ordinary things in rooms, levels, ruins and caves, made from the seed and never stored, to pick up and carry, eat, light or sell; taking in someone's house is seen; some old things are secretly valuable until appraised. Music: a harpsichord, Baroque dances and a tune of its own for every kind of place, and pieces for a discovery, a win and a death. Fixes: thin outlines only round the outside of people (no masks on faces), caves that fade into the distance, less shimmer far off. See `JOURNAL.md` Also removes the ink lines over everything in other worlds (from commission 6): other worlds keep their own palettes and style, without the lines. |
| v0.19 | Commission 8: character. The ground has eight kinds you can tell apart at a glance (grass, sand, rock, snow, earth, gravel, mud, salt crust) with crisp lined edges, no visible tiling, patches of lush and dry grass, bare earth and flowers. Roads look made: cobbled streets with kerbs near towns, the lost people's old slab roads half under the turf, rutted tracks with waymarks, bridges over rivers; fields with walls, hedges or fences round every town. Rooms are furnished by what they are for: real floors (they were hidden under the building's plinth), rooms by wealth, altars with the god's own shrine and pews, tables laid, pots by the hearth, abandoned houses broken and cobwebbed, poor and rich beds, tables, chairs and chests. High pushed much further (more land, deeper grass, stones and leaves underfoot, finer ground); Low and Medium as cheap as before. Fixes: the traveller at the fire answers, and hamlets on the land are small towns you walk into. See `JOURNAL.md` |
| v0.7 | Housekeeping: tests behind one command, named random streams, versioned saves with copy and paste between devices, ARCHITECTURE.md, version shown in Menu |

Versions 0.3 to 0.5 were built before the project was kept in git; their features are all in v0.6.
